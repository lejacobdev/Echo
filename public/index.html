This browser can't run Echo

How it works

  1. Both phones open Echo and connect (or pick roles in nearby mode).
  2. Hold the phones together once and calibrate.
  3. Walk apart — ping to see the live distance shrink as you close in.

              About Echo

              Echo measures the round-trip time of ultrasonic chirps between two phones and converts it to distance. Everything acoustic happens on-device.

              About Echo

              Echo's Seeker phone plays a short, near-inaudible chirp and starts a timer. The Responder phone hears it and instantly chirps back on a different frequency. The Seeker measures the round trip, subtracts the hardware latency you calibrated away, and converts the remainder to distance at the speed of sound (343 m/s).

              Honest limitations

              • Accuracy is realistically 1–3 m — browser audio buffering adds noise. It's a proximity aid, not survey gear.
              • Range is roughly 5–10 m in quiet conditions; loud or echoey places reduce it.
              • Distance only, no compass bearing — that would need synchronized multi-mic access, a native-app stretch goal.
              • Some laptops and older phones can't emit or hear 19–20 kHz well — try frequency channel B in settings.

              Privacy

              The microphone runs only while a find session is open and its state is always shown in the header. Audio is analyzed on-device and never recorded, stored or uploaded. Ultrasound got a bad name from covert ad-tracking beacons — Echo is the opposite: strictly opt-in, visible and session-only.
